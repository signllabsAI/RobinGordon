Three gaps between your reputation and your signal

We did not run the full audit here. This is a surface scan of what is publicly visible to the engines. Three things stood out, and every one of them is fixable.

FINDING 01 · ENTITY COLLISION

The engines can't tell which Robin Gordon you are

A quick search surfaces at least four real estate professionals named Robin Gordon across the country, including agents in California and Delaware. When someone asks an AI about "Robin Gordon, realtor," the model has to guess which one holds the $3 billion Main Line record. Right now, nothing in your public footprint makes that answer obvious. Your authority is getting split across strangers who share your name.

FINDING 02 · STORY MISALIGNMENT

Your own numbers don't agree, so the machines stay vague

Across your public sources, career sales are stated as $1.34 billion, $2.25 billion, and $3 billion in different places. A human reads that as "a lot." An answer engine reads conflicting facts and does the safe thing: it says nothing specific, or it hedges. Consistency is not a branding nicety here. It is the difference between an engine naming your number and refusing to.

FINDING 03 · A STRONG SIGNAL, POORLY WIRED

You have the fuel. It just isn't feeding the engines.

WSJ, the Inquirer, RealTrends, Patch, a real website, an active team page. This is exactly the earned authority that AI citations are built from, and most agents don't have it. The problem is that it lives as human stories, not as the structured, cross linked, machine readable facts the engines trust. The raw material is rare. The wiring is missing.
